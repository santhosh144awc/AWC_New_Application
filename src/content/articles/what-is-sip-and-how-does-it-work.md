A **Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)** is an investment method offered by mutual funds that allows you to invest a fixed amount of money at regular intervals (typically monthly) into a chosen mutual fund scheme.

Rather than waiting to accumulate a large lumpsum of capital, an SIP automates your savings discipline directly from your bank account.

## The Two Core Mechanisms of an SIP

### 1. Rupee Cost Averaging (Spreading Purchases Over Time)
When you invest through a monthly SIP, you purchase fund units across varying market cycles:
* When the market is **falling**, NAV is lower, and your fixed monthly instalment purchases **more units**.
* When the market is **rising**, NAV is higher, and your fixed instalment purchases **fewer units**.

Rupee Cost Averaging does not lower the stock market's underlying volatility or eliminate market risk. Rather, it spreads your purchase price over time and removes the anxiety of trying to time market highs and lows.

### 2. The Compounding Multiplier
Compounding occurs when the earnings generated by your investments generate their own earnings over multi-year horizons.

For example, contributing ₹10,000 at the beginning of each month for 15 years (180 months) at an assumed 12% effective annual return (EAR):
* **Total Amount Invested:** ₹18,00,000
* **Estimated Future Corpus:** ₹47,59,314
* **Estimated Compound Wealth Gain:** ₹29,59,314

## What is a Step-Up SIP?

A **Step-Up (or Top-Up) SIP** allows you to increase your monthly investment periodically (for example, by 10% each year) as your income grows.

Stepping up your SIP dramatically expands your accumulated wealth because you steadily save more capital alongside compounding. For instance, contributing ₹10,000/month at an illustrative 12% effective annual return over 20 years results in an estimated corpus of approximately **₹92.0 Lakhs** with a fixed SIP, compared to approximately **₹1.86 Crore** if stepped up by 10% annually. The higher corpus reflects both the extra savings you contribute over time and the compound earnings on those additional contributions.

## Important Takeaways

1. An SIP is a disciplined method of investing, not an independent asset class.
2. Rupee cost averaging spreads purchases across market cycles; it does not eliminate equity market risk or guarantee against losses.
3. Starting early gives compound interest more time to work—the duration of your investment horizon is the single most powerful factor in wealth creation.
